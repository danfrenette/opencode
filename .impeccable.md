## Design Context

### Users
This fork is used by a single power user working on serious software development tasks. The interface supports minute-to-minute and second-to-second work, especially inside the session composer where prompts are written, adjusted, and sent continuously.

### Brand Personality
Intentional, out of the way, clean.

The product should feel calm, precise, and dependable under heavy daily use. It should help work disappear into the background rather than constantly advertising itself.

### Aesthetic Direction
Premium and refined, with a low-noise visual language.

The UI should avoid a dashboard-cluster feel, avoid crowded control surfaces, and avoid over-signaling productivity. The preferred direction is composed and editorial rather than flashy: restrained hierarchy, compact controls, thoughtful spacing, and surfaces that feel deliberate instead of busy.

### Design Principles
1. Keep the primary task visually dominant and let secondary controls recede.
2. Favor one coherent surface over fragmented rows, cards, or clusters.
3. Use restraint as a quality signal: fewer outlines, quieter contrast, tighter copy.
4. Optimize for repeated use; high-frequency components should feel effortless, not attention-seeking.
5. Polish should come from spacing, alignment, and hierarchy rather than decorative effects.
